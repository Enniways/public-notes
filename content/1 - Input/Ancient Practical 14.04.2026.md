# Eudaimonia

alias The good Life
Happiness, Fortune in ancient greek
_(sort of like a positive deamon)_
Basically used as equivalent to **success**

There is a strong connection between `[[Virtue]]` and happiness - basically the same thing in **common** ancient greek society

## In Plato's Dialogues

The search for virtue is not really distinguished from the search for virtue. The best life is a virtues one

### But justice might preclude happiness

#### reaction 1: Callicles, Thrasymachus

They reject the standards of justice (and other virtues), saying that, basically, they are _not virtues, but vices_

#### reaction 2: Socrates

rejects the **conception of happiness that renders justice not admirable** and not beneficial
_(→ justice makes u happy)_

## But why isn't justice admirable (kalon) but sometimes not beneficial (agathon)?

### Modern ethics

moral goodness is different from living a good life

### Plato

Plato is not unaware of this possibility but rejects it - he does not see a conception of morality divvorced from a living good life as viable - in the same way in which it does not make sense to say that one can have a healthy body and yet lack physical well-being, so it does not seem to him sensible to say that one can be virtuous and yet live a bad life

# Homer

## Virtue in Homer (excellence)

virtue or excellence is a **quality of a warrior** (particularly warrior king or chieftain) that depends on his reputation or fame, his fight prowess shown in battle, his property, and the subjects he rules. It is primarily an **aristocratic male quality**

In this sense, virtue is a competitive quality - in aiming to the best one aims to outdo others. This Peleus famously urges Achilles to 'always be the best (aristeuein) and prevail over others' (Illiad 11.784)

# Competitive and Cooperative Virtue

We have city-states with rich internal political lives.
It is this new context, that of the Greek polis which creates virtue of 'the
human (anthrōpinē) and political (politikê) kind'
Within polis, you need to cooperate

- Competitive virtue - you have to be better than others. **Only a few can be**
- Cooperative virtue - you should excel as you should be. **All should have it**

**Those two aren't well together** and aren't differentiated in the greek mind.

Common people still think competitively and go to `[[Sophist|Sophists]]` to learn

`[[Socrates]]` appeals to their "moral intuitions" that reflect a **cooperative conception**, in order to reveal the contradictions
_Example_: Thrasymachus claims that a virtuos person is one who outdoes others, Socrates shows him that justice precludes outdoing others because that is abad thing - hence, a virtuous person should not be outdoing anyone

Protagoras avoids this trap by saying, the Sophists only teach people who already know what justice is → `[[Protagoras' Great Speech]]`

## Competitive view

- "the superior should take by force what belongs to the inferior, that the better should rule ther worse and the more worthy have a greater share than the less worthy" (448b - Socrates' summary of Callicles' view) in `[[Gorgias]]` Dialogue
  → The fact that i can take it makes it mine
  **That is just!**

## The Cooperative view

The life of the person who respects, even reveres the norma of equality and justice and pursues worldly success only with those norms, valuing the norms above all else
