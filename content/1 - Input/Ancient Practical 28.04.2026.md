# Virtue according to Socrates

- Socrates has arguments that, in one way or another, show that **kowldege or wisdom (epistēmē, sophia, etc.) are required for virtue**
- He recognizes that **all virtues serve us to overcome adversity**, bad luck, or deficiencies of nature - which is a function they share with craft (technē) → `[[Apology of Socrates]]` → `[[Expert vs Non-expert Knowledge]]`

# Virtue requires Knowledge according to Socrates

Eythydemus 278e-282a:

1. We all want to do well and be happy
2. What is doing well/happiness? Possessing good things (wealth, health, beauty, honor, virtues, good fortune)
3. But good fortune befalls more likely those with knowledge (say sailors, who know navigation are more likey to be lucky)
4. Similarly **all other good things are not good for a person unless they posses wisdom, and that wisdom is what makes them good**
5. Why? It is not the possession but the use of such things that benefits a person and in order to benefit one must use these things properly.
6. Hence, to be happy, **one needs knowledge of how to properly use the usual 'goods'.**

# Virtue According to Sophists

## Protagoras on Virtue in `[[Protagoras Dialogue]]`

→ `[[Socrates questioning Protagoras about the Unity of the Virtues]]`

- Protagoras promises to teach his students to be able to deliberate well about private and public affairs. This ability, he thinks will best position them to succesd in life
- He thinks this is **largely a matter of learning to reason correctly**.
- When he speaks of knowledge of good an d bad, he has in mind knowledge of things that are useful or beneficial in promoting one's goals and aspriations. His teaching enables one to discover these things
- Protagoras thinks virtue is acquired through social conditioning (like language) but the skill or reasoning correctly that he taeches makes one able to translate it effectively into actions and words.
